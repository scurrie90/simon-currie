# Vestigial Tribalism, Hard Men and Sports

## We Used To Go To War

Until the baby boomer generation, for most of  human history, most societies have had at least one conflict per generation that mobilized and killed a large chunk of its young men. Without getting into the purpose or function of such conflicts (geopolitically and sociologically), this was essentially an accepted fact. If you went to a Saloon in San Francisco in 1905 or a coffee shop in Vienna in 1935, the vast majority of men over 30 in those rooms had seen combat. A plurality, if not a majority, had killed someone personally. 

## Men Were Aggressive

This capacity for violence wasn't confined to war and combat - men used to settle interpersonal disputes with violence at much higher rate than they do now, it was part and parcel to being "a man". For men under about 25, this actually continued into the boomer generation and didn't end until Gen X. "Boys will be boys" - schoolyard fights were treated like hockey fights, not "allowed", but not an egregious offence either. "5 minutes each for fighting." Getting into a bar fight in your late teens or early twenties wasn't confined to tough guys from the rougher parts of town. The data on violent crime backs this up - in the developed world, violent crime is down across the board. This is ostensibly a good thing - one could even describe it as progress. And the trickle effect is an indisputable good: less violent men means safer women. Domestic disputes, stranger sexual assaults, and so forth are all part of the overall decline in violent crime. 

Men weren't just hard in their capacity for, and willingness to do, violence. The era(s) in question were lacking a social safety net - the further back in the time, the lesser the safety net. Men were self-reliant *because they had to be or they died.* Between starvation, disease, and violence (either combat or interpersonal), there were not a lot of "soft men" left at age 30. The meek and timid did not make it that long. The natural selection was such that by and large the men that populated society were hard people, self-reliant, willing to do violence, understanding that help would not be arriving. And largely suspicious of outsiders.

## Tribalism Was Survival

This suspicion of outsiders was well-founded - historically, outsiders have caused societies to *at best* change and at worst, collapse. It was also primordial, part of their genetic makeup - a function of the lizard brain. Tribalism has existed as long as societies themselves have existed. It exists in all primates and most mammals. It serves an important function in terms of resource conservation and social preservation. It is the original conservative position. 

Much like the neutering of male aggression in the late 20th century, tribalism fell out of vogue in the neoliberal global community, and in the West in particular, at about the same time. While the 2010s and 2020s have seen an open revival of classic tribalism - White Christian Nationalism in the US, Poland, Hungary and throughout Western Europe. Anti immigration sentiment across the west is at an all time high. The United States is deporting migrants to ghost jails in El Salvador without due process. But in polite and educated circles, this is considered regressive and harmful. And it is likely both, however, both tribalism and aggression served a purpose, and continue to serve a purpose, whether it's polite to say so or not. Without addressing the purposes they serve, any pontification regarding the morality of violence or xenophobia or anything else is moot.

## The Agreeable Global Community

In the wake of World War II, despite the ever looming threat of nuclear holocaust from the cold war, it was generally accepted that diplomacy, not warfare, was the way forward in geopolitical disputes and conflicts (unless you're the United States and your opponent is some peasants on rice paddies). The old colonial empires of Britain, France, Belgium, Portugal, etc dissolved, and mass global migration occured. The various "tribes" become variably integrated. 

At the same time, global economic output soared. By the 1980s, we produced enough crap globally for everyone to have a lower middle class North American lifestyle. While we have a distribution problem (and a wealth hoarding problem, but that's a different post), that remains the case. We essentially live in a post-scarcity society if we want to - for now. Because we don't need to invade each other for resources, and because we've agreed that industrial scale warfare is far too destructive to engage in regularly, the post-industrial, late-capitalist global community had become a far more agreeable place. Very recent events aside (also another post), immigration was generally encouraged, trade was generally free, economic unions became common place. The geopolitical arena went from zero-sum to net-positive, at least according to liberal political theorists.

This has permeated the culture in North America and Western Europe. Because violence as means of conflict resolution is frowned upon, great care is taken not to give too much offence, or, when giving a great deal of offence, to use an appeal to authority (in this case, societal rules) to avoid facing any meaningful consequences. We're all supposed to be "agreeable" and when we are not an "agreeable sort", we are socially ostracized (this is irrelevant of violence - if one strays outside the bounds of acceptable opinions/conversation/etc, there are social consequences). Because any degree of genuine tribalism is thrown out as racism/xenophobia/bigotry, any conversation about resources and capacity is eschewn. 

## Vestigial Tribalism and Why It Matters

The fact of the matter is that our tribalism and our aggression has served a functional purpose since before *Homo Sapiens Sapiens*. We likely genocided the Neanderthals, and probably not out of boredome. The "uncanny valley" is a well understood biological phenomena. And no amount of liberal political progress is going to override an evolutionary mechanism that ingrained, not for thousands of years anyway. So we needed to channel it, and we've done so somewhat successfully, but to our detriment. 

The coincidental rise of major sports is not actually a coincidence. It's a direct effect - without war, without tribalism, with a large social safety net, young men needed an outlet. An outlet for that aggression, but especially, for that inherent tribalism. Nowhere is this direct relationship with war more apparent than gridiron football, where uniformed men "take the field", one team attacks, one defends, with the goal of marching the entire field. And nowhere is the direct relationship with tribalism more apparent than associatian football in Europe, or, more specifically, *association football fans.* Firms, ultras, groups of young men from across the class spectrum - many are young professionals, these aren't hoodlums *per se* - getting together and engaging in violence based on their allegiance to a sports club. 

In association football "firms", there is often a political undercurrent as well. Many are far right, but some are notoriously left-wing and working-class (the InterCity Firm of Westham United was generally "left leaning" in the 1980s, for example). This again, isn't a coincidence - in the early 21st century, we've seen the rise of a different tribe. Closer to the original ones, where there are real stakes as opposed sports club scores. The political tribe has become so tangible that if we were to see a regression into the serious violence of our forefathers, it would likely be on those lines.

## That's Not Good

Here's the thing: the reality of the 21st century is that we're about to experience real scarcity and with it, real competition for resources. That means the liberal, net-positive politics of the latter half of the 20th century not only do not apply, but are a detrimental denial of the facts on the ground. *Realpolitik* will be the modus operandi as energy scarcity pervades, and then is quickly surprassed by water scarcity. Globally, different tribes are going to compete for limited resources.

By ostracizing anyone who has an aggressive instinct outside the acceptable sports and digital politics arenas, who recognizes that we shouldn't be sharing resources just because we happen to have them right now (which is debatable anyway), we put ourselves in a position of weakness. The hard among us are the ones who can practically fight those conflicts, but being "hard" is ostracized, often pushed to a different tribe (you see this in cultural leftist circles cancelling valuable members for punching a nazi - always a moral good.) 

I quite like that Gen Z is incredibly nice to each other, embracing the progress millennials did right while pushing back against some of the more nonsensical elements of my generation's culture war - it will be my next article. But I wouldn't want these pussies fighting for my water rights: they are soft as baby shit. Every conflict they've ever had has been verbal, often not even in person but online. They've been raised in a society that directs any aggression to sports and video games, and records their every move such that they're scared to ever step out of society's lines. But it's by stepping out of those lines you learn who's made of glass and who isn't. They're neutered - it's not even that they "can't fight", that can be taught. It's that they don't have the aggression, it's been entirely pushed out of the vast majority of them. 

## There Is A Progressive Way Out

Despite all of the above, there is actually a net-positive, progressive way forward that does not rely on Tribalism or Aggression making a 21st century come back. I find it unlikely to come to fruition given the facts on the ground, which is why I wrote the above, but it is possible. The solution is solving nuclear fusion, which would end energy which in turn ends water scarcity (because desalination and water creation via fusion are both possible, just incredibly energy intensive). The issue is that funding this research is the realm of a very few organizations' budgets, and that research would nulify the concept of "budget" in the first place. Altering the status quo so drastically is not in the interest of any entity that could afford the research. I'll discuss this option - ironically, the nuclear option - in a later post.
